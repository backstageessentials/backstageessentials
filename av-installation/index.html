Title and subtitle

AV Installation: Fundamentals for Audiovisual Integrators and Installers

Synopsis

A person will be prepared to walk onto a jobsite, read drawings, and install audio, video, and control systems, and understand networking concepts well enough to make the systems work together. The book covers installation, not design. It assumes the system has been designed, and the drawings are in hand.

The book covers the different cable types and their uses, and the tools required to properly install audio, video, and control systems in corporate environments and performance venues. Fifteen chapters covering the how to of installation. Objectives, quizzes, and skill sheets for on-the-job training or educational settings.

Why this book

This book explores what an AV installation technician is and the work they do in the AV industry. The many descriptions within the AV industry refer to integrator, installer, technician, and field tech, and they do not all mean the same thing. An AV installation technician is the person who will install various AV systems for a variety of venues. This is what this book covers, that person.

The book will have competency-based skill sheets. They can be used in a classroom, for training, or for personal use. The skill sheets will give you the steps needed to perform each competency.

The book came from me teaching the AV installation course at Laurus College. I found, just as I did with my previous book Backstage Essentials, that there is no book for a beginner in commercial AV installation, and most books concentrate on the design aspect. My book is not design.

Chapter outline

Each chapter includes Chapter Objectives, Competencies, End of Chapter Summary, Skill Sheets, and References.

CH 01
The job
Explore what the job of an audiovisual installation technician is. Roles on the jobsite and who does what. Working alongside other trades. Jobsite etiquette, soft skills, and knowing when to stop and ask questions rather than guess. Describes how to come from live events to working audiovisual installation projects.
CH 02
Tools and the kit
How do you put together a tool kit for the job? What tools are essential, what is provided to you, and what you need to own. Safe handling of each tool, without teaching the techniques that come later in the book. Finally, a review of each tool and its common uses.
Competencies. Assemble a basic AV installation tool kit; Identify common AV installation tools and their uses; Inspect tools for damage before use; Demonstrate safe handling of each tool.
CH 03
Safety, codes, and the AHJ
Working on the jobsite requires following safety procedures that protect you and the jobsite. Reviews the common codes that need to be followed and understanding the Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ) and what that means for your AV work each day. Personal protective equipment (PPE) and working at heights. Ending with what happens if the inspection fails and what to do next.
Competencies. Set up and work safely from a ladder; Select and wear appropriate personal protective equipment for the task.
CH 04
Reading the documents
There are many documents for the AV installation technician. Explore the drawing set, such as floor plans, elevations, and reflected ceiling plans. Understanding, reading, and comprehending signal flow diagrams, the bill of materials, RFIs, and change forms. Who to ask questions to, and how to interpret the many symbols on these drawings.
Competencies. Locate and identify the sheets in a drawing set; Interpret symbols on a floor plan and reflected ceiling plan; Trace a signal path through a signal flow diagram; Verify equipment against a bill of materials; Complete an RFI.
CH 05
Cable and connectors
This chapter explores the many different types of cables and connectors used in the audiovisual world, including how to terminate cable connections, testing, labeling standards from AVIXA (F501.01:2015, Cable Labeling for Audiovisual Systems), and maintaining clean cable runs. Detailed procedures on how to terminate cables, with skill sheets for practice.
Competencies. Identify cable types and select the correct one for the application; Strip cable without damaging the conductors; Terminate a copper connector; Test and verify a terminated cable; Label cable to AVIXA F501 standard; Dress and secure a cable run.
CH 06
Cable Pulling
This chapter explores how to prepare cable to be pulled from point A to point B. Plenum spaces and what is allowed in them. Pull technique, including bend radius, tension, pull strings, and separation from power. Recognize when to stop and ask for guidance before committing to a pull.
Competencies. Plan a cable route; Install and use a pull string; Attach cable to a pull string; Pull cable from point A to point B.
CH 07
Mounting and structure
Reviewing wall and ceiling types and what each will hold, along with verifying structural backing and blocking in walls, fasteners, display and projector mounts, and weight ratings and safety factors for equipment. What to do when the backing is missing or in the wrong place.
Competencies. Identify wall and ceiling types and their load capacity; Verify structural backing before mounting; Select the correct fastener for the substrate; Install a display mount; Install a projector mount.
CH 08
Racks
Why are racks used in the audiovisual industry, along with rack hardware and accessories. Covers weight distribution, cooling a rack, rack power, and fastening equipment to the rack. Also explores signal separation, RF and IR, and cable slack. When does the rack get built, shop versus site, and advantages and disadvantages to both.
Competencies. Plan a rack layout and equipment placement; Mount equipment in a rack; Manage power and signal cable inside a rack; Maintain signal separation within a rack; Dress and label rack cabling; Verify airflow and thermal management.
CH 09
Power and grounding
Understanding the basics of power and grounding for audiovisual systems. An installer will verify a circuit, meter outlets and equipment, and trace a ground loop. Branch circuits and distribution are the electrician's work, and this chapter explains what the AV installation technician needs to understand about them.
Competencies. Verify a circuit; Meter an outlet and equipment; Trace a ground loop.
CH 10
Signal transport
Reviews how a signal gets to where it needs to get to. HDMI and its distance limits, HDBaseT, SDI, fiber and when you need it. USB extension. Analog and digital audio, speaker level versus line level, and the things that break these systems. Teaches the reader to take SPL measurements to verify system coverage. EDID, HDCP, and troubleshooting.
Competencies. Select the correct transport method for a given distance and signal type; Install and verify an HDBaseT link; Connect and verify a video signal path; Distinguish speaker level from line level and connect accordingly; Take SPL measurements to verify system coverage; Troubleshoot an EDID or HDCP failure.
CH 11
Networked AV
AV networking covers switches for AV, VLANs, IP addressing basics, and PoE. Covers bandwidth for audio and video systems, what uncompressed video does to a network, latency, and the common AV over IP platforms that are available. The installer will learn how to connect and verify networked AV based on the design parameters.
Competencies. Connect AV devices to a network switch; Assign and verify an IP address on an AV device; Verify PoE delivery to a device; Confirm an AV device is on the correct VLAN; Verify a networked AV stream is passing.
CH 12
Control systems
Control systems are what make your customer happy. This chapter will explore processors and touch panels, how they work, and how they can make things easier for the AV system. Also includes serial, IR, relay and IP, mounting and connecting, and programming the control system. Programming is typically designing work, but the installation technician will end up using the file to make adjustments, add functionality the customer requested, or to troubleshoot.
Competencies. Install and connect a control processor; Mount and connect a touch panel; Wire serial, IR, and relay control connections; Load a control program to a processor; Make an adjustment to an existing control program; Test each button on the touch panel and confirm the correct response.
CH 13
Installing corporate spaces
This chapter explores the uniqueness of installing AV systems in the corporate space. Approaching how to install systems in a conference room including ceiling microphone arrays, PTZ camera placement, table boxes, and power considerations. Working with and combining divisible rooms. Setting up and installing video conference equipment in smaller rooms for meetings.
Competencies. Install a ceiling microphone array; Install and position a PTZ camera for a conference room; Install a table box and terminate its connections; Install and connect video conferencing equipment; Configure and verify a divisible room combining function.
CH 14
Installing in performance venues
As an AV installation technician you will be working in performance spaces, such as theatres and houses of worship, and working around rigging and catwalks. This chapter will explore those spaces and what is required to install in them. How to estimate longer cable runs, access to the space, connecting to existing equipment, and safety for both scissor lifts and catwalks. Look at what typical lighting and sound systems look like in a performance space, and what elements are similar between corporate and performance venues.
Competencies. Operate a scissor lift safely; Work safely from a catwalk; Estimate and pull a long cable run in a venue; Connect new equipment to an existing house system; Identify tie lines and patch points in a venue.
CH 15
Commissioning and handover
This chapter starts with testing and verifying the systems you have installed. Reviewing the documents, punch lists, as-built drawings for the customer, and labeling verification. Finishing with client training, closeout documentation and warranties, and the handoff to the customer.
Competencies. Test a completed system against the drawings; Create and work a punch list; Verify labeling against the as-built documents; Mark up drawings to as-built condition; Train the client on system operation; Assemble a closeout package.
END MATTER

Who it is for

This book is for anyone interested in the AV profession, including stagehand technicians looking to go into installation. Also, for technicians who do install work for AV companies, low voltage companies, and alarm companies.

Primary audience: AV installation technician, AV integration specialist, installation technician. The only course I found is AUD 320 AV Installation at Laurus College, and I teach that course.

Secondary audience: anyone interested in learning more about AV installation in corporate spaces and performance venues.

The NSCA reports that the average member has at least seven open positions at any time, which they put at 17,000 nationally, and their 2026 State of the Industry survey found 78 percent of integrators naming talent as their biggest challenge. Every one of those hires needs training. That is the pool. How many of them buy a book is not something I can estimate.

What it fixes

There are three issues this book will solve.

STANDARD
No occupational standard. The UK folds AV installation into a broad events technician apprenticeship at Level 5. Canada classifies AV work under electronics repair or engineering technology. The US has certifications but no occupational standard. Those certifications fall under AVIXA with the CTS, CTS-I, and ANP, CEDIA with the CIT and IST, and ETA International with credentials such as the Commercial Audio Technician.
COURSE
No course specific to AV installation that I could find, except AUD 320 at Laurus College, which I teach.
BOOK
No book, except one that is out of print.

The book resolves those issues by providing an up-to-date book on current technologies, with competencies, skill sheets, and coverage of installation start to finish. The competencies give the work a defined set of skills where no occupational standard exists.

Pedagogy

Competency-based education. I will have skill sheets at the end of each chapter for the reader to complete and check off after performing the skill. The advantage of this type of approach is that each reader will be able to verify whether they understand the skill. If used in a classroom, the instructor can document that they can perform the skill. The skill sheets assume access to equipment through a classroom, an employer, or an apprenticeship. A reader working alone uses them as a checklist to complete on the job.

Pedagogy will have learning objectives, competencies, end of chapter summaries, quizzes, skill sheets, references, an end of book glossary, and the appendix checklist. The competencies stay in the chapters. The appendix is a checklist of all of them in one place.

I teach AUD 320 AV Installation at Laurus College. The course runs a five-week term starting next week and I am building it around this material. It will be complete before the review process finishes, and I will use the book in class to test it in future terms.

There are four books that overlap with my book. The CIT book covers safety, tools, cable, and termination at entry level, which is my chapters 2 through 6. The CTS-I book covers documentation, cable, mounting, racks, networks, control, and closeout, which is most of my book. Where my book is distinguished is that it covers racks, signal transport, networking, control, and commissioning together in one book aimed at the beginner in commercial and performance spaces. The CIT ends at infrastructure. The IST book is residential. The CTS-I assumes you have been working in the field. The 2009 handbook is unavailable and predates HDMI at distance, HDBaseT, EDID, HDCP, PoE, and AV over IP.

All four books are organized around a particular organization's certification. The two CEDIA books follow exam blueprints. The CTS-I follows exam objectives. The 2009 handbook has an old version of the current CTS-I exam outline. My book follows the job, from arriving on the jobsite through handover.

The three current books all have chapter practice questions based on the exams they cover, glossaries, and color graphics. Mine has those too, and competencies and skill sheets. Nobody currently has those. The reader performs the task and checks it off, and an instructor or training manager can document it.

Audience level is beginning, and the book is not written for study toward a certification exam. Writing style is direct and to the point. Artwork will be photos and line art.
