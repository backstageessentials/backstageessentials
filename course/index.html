Backstage Essentials: Foundations of Live Event Production is built and not yet open for enrolment. This sheet describes what it contains.

Live event work is mostly learned on the job. The crew around you is busy building a show against a deadline, and nobody has time to explain the basics. The course explains them first, following a show from the call to the last case on the truck.

The six units

No.UnitCovers
1Professional FoundationThe roles on a crew, radio call and response, PPE, safe lifting, what to bring, and how to act on the floor.
2PreproductionReading a call sheet, planning your arrival, and knowing which show document holds what.
3Load InReading the venue, handling road cases, fall zones, forklifts and lifts, and the order of a load in.
4Systems BuildAudio, lighting, video, and rigging gear by sight, tracing a signal, line check, and soundcheck.
5Show DayCues, headset and radio discipline, changeovers, emergencies, and working around performers.
6Strike and WrapStrike order, coiling and packing, reporting damaged gear, and the habits that get you called back.

What is in it

LESSONS
70 written lessons, each with a practical exercise and a self check. Images and diagrams throughout. No video or audio.
KNOWLEDGE CHECKS
A scenario based knowledge check at the end of every unit.
PRACTICE
Seven interactive practice activities, including the Analog Mixer Trainer.
FINAL
100 questions drawn at random from a bank of 400. Ninety minutes, 75 percent to pass, two retakes.
LENGTH
About 12 hours of work, self paced. Three to four weeks at three to four hours a week is the suggested pace.
ON PASSING
A certificate of completion. It is not a certification and not an industry credential.
PLATFORM
LearnWorlds.
PRICE
$149 introductory through 1 April 2027, then $199. One time purchase.

Who it is for

You are sixteen or older. You have no live event experience and you do not need any. You read English.

Aspiring stagehands, overhire crew, IATSE apprentices waiting on a first dispatch, high school students looking at the trade, theater technicians, volunteers, and career changers.

What it is not

It is not a substitute for hands on experience, an apprenticeship, or union training. It is the foundation that helps you arrive prepared.
